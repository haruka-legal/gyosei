A gyoseishoshi (sometimes translated as "administrative scrivener") is a licensed legal professional in Japan. Within the scope permitted by law, a gyoseishoshi prepares documents for government offices and handles application procedures. A gyoseishoshi is not the same as an attorney.<br>
The scope of a gyoseishoshi's work is said to cover more than 10,000 types of documents. As a result, individual gyoseishoshi tend to specialize in particular areas. One of these areas is immigration, including applications for a status of residence submitted to the Immigration Services Agency of Japan. Your status of residence determines how long you may stay in Japan and what activities you are allowed to carry out, such as working or studying. It is often informally called a "visa," although the two are legally different.<br>
The Japan Federation of Certified Administrative Procedures Legal Specialists Associations, the national body for gyoseishoshi, also explains what gyoseishoshi do for foreign residents on its [English page](https://www.gyosei.or.jp/about/language/english).

<h2 id="how-to-choose">How to Choose a Gyoseishoshi</h2>

There are about 56,000 gyoseishoshi in Japan, and about 8,900 in Tokyo alone (as of October 1, 2026). Each one has different strengths and a different way of working, so choosing the right one matters. Before you hire one, check the following points.

- **Do they handle immigration procedures?** Each gyoseishoshi specializes in different areas. Check whether they handle status of residence applications and whether they can help with your situation.
- **Are they experienced and attentive?** Along with experience in immigration procedures, it matters whether they explain things clearly and keep you updated along the way. Someone who takes even simple questions seriously will give you peace of mind.
- **Does their approach suit you?** Some people want to hand everything over and get it done quickly. Others prefer to discuss each step and take their time. Choose someone whose way of working matches what you want.
- **Consider where you live now, or plan to live.** Hiring a gyoseishoshi in the area where you live, or plan to live, can make it easier to consult them about future procedures such as renewals. However, there is no rule that you must hire one in a particular area. Some offices also offer online consultations to clients anywhere in Japan or abroad.
- **Are the fees clear?** Check whether there are costs beyond the basic fee, such as extra charges, translation costs or government fees. It is also a good idea to ask in advance what happens if your application is denied, and what the refund conditions are.
- **Do they promise approval?** Be careful of anyone who guarantees that your application will be approved.
- **Are they really a gyoseishoshi?** In principle, the law prohibits individuals or businesses without the required license or legal authority from preparing application documents as a paid professional service. You can check whether someone is registered in the [online directory of registered gyoseishoshi](https://www.gyosei.or.jp/members-search) on the website of the Japan Federation of Certified Administrative Procedures Legal Specialists Associations (in Japanese).

Some offices offer a free first consultation or a free initial review of your situation. Using these opportunities to compare several offices is a good idea. Compare not only the fees, but also how clearly they explain things and your impression of the gyoseishoshi, and choose someone you truly trust.

<h2 id="benefits">Benefits of Hiring a Gyoseishoshi</h2>

In principle, you can apply for a status of residence on your own. Hiring a gyoseishoshi is not required. You can also choose to consult a gyoseishoshi for advice only and then handle the procedure yourself.<br>
Even so, hiring a gyoseishoshi has these benefits.

- **A professional assessment of your situation** Before you apply, a gyoseishoshi can review your situation and the requirements for approval, and help identify possible problems and what you need to prepare.
- **Help planning for your future** You can discuss which status of residence suits your current job and family situation, and plan ahead for future job changes, marriage, bringing your family to Japan or permanent residence.
- **The right documents for your case** A gyoseishoshi can confirm which documents and explanations your situation requires.
- **Help with Japanese paperwork** You can leave the preparation of Japanese application forms and supporting documents to a professional.
- **Fewer trips to the immigration office** Gyoseishoshi who have completed the required training and notified the immigration authorities can submit certain immigration applications on behalf of their clients. For eligible procedures, you generally do not need to visit the immigration office yourself, although you may still be asked to come in person.
- **Help with applications from abroad** When you live overseas, contacting immigration or collecting documents issued in Japan can be difficult. A gyoseishoshi can work with your employer or family in Japan when needed and help you prepare your application remotely.
- **Confidential advice** Gyoseishoshi are legally required to keep confidential any information they learn through their work, so you can safely discuss personal matters such as your income or family situation.

<h2 id="limitations">Limitations of Hiring a Gyoseishoshi</h2>

- **It costs money** You need to pay the gyoseishoshi's fee. Depending on the procedure, government fees and the cost of obtaining documents may be charged separately.
- **Approval is not guaranteed** The immigration authorities decide whether to approve or deny your application. Hiring a gyoseishoshi does not change the requirements for approval.
- **It may not be faster** Preparation may go more smoothly, but the time immigration authorities take to review your application will not necessarily be shorter.
- **Your cooperation is still needed** Some documents and information can only come from you, so you cannot leave everything to the gyoseishoshi.
- **They may not be able to take your case** For example, if your request goes beyond what a gyoseishoshi is permitted to handle, they may not be able to accept it.

Even if one office turns you down, another office may be able to help, or they may refer you to a more suitable professional. Please feel free to reach out.
