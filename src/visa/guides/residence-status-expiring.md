**In short:** If your period of stay is about to expire, the most important thing is to apply to immigration by the expiry date.<br>
If your period of stay is 6 months or longer, you can apply for an extension from 3 months before it expires.<br>
If you apply by the expiry date, you can stay in Japan for a certain period even if the date passes while your application is under review.<br>
If your period of stay expires without an application, you become an overstayer. Contact immigration immediately.

<h2 id="check">First, check your expiry date</h2>

Your expiry date is on the front of your residence card, in the "Period of Stay (Date of Expiration)" field.

As soon as you notice, check how many days you have left and start preparing right away.

<h2 id="extension">You can apply for an extension from 3 months before expiry</h2>

If you will continue the same activities, apply for an **Extension of Period of Stay (在留期間更新許可申請)**.

- When to apply: if your period of stay is 6 months or longer, from 3 months before expiry up to the expiry date
- Processing time: the Immigration Services Agency's guideline is 2 weeks to 1 month. In practice, it depends on your status of residence and your application
- How to apply: at a Regional Immigration Services Bureau counter, or online (online applications have eligibility conditions)
- Fee: paid when your application is approved. The amount depends on the period of stay granted. For 1 year, it is ¥33,000 if you apply at the counter (for applications received on or after October 1, 2026)

You can apply on the expiry date itself, but if documents are missing, your application may not be accepted. Prepare early.

<h2 id="special-period">Apply by the expiry date and you can stay during review (the special period)</h2>

If your period of stay is longer than 30 days and you apply for an extension or change by the expiry date, you can keep staying in Japan for a certain period even if your original expiry date passes during the review. This is called the **special period (特例期間)**.

You can stay until whichever of the following comes first:

- The time a decision, such as approval or denial, is made on your application
- The end of the day 2 months after your original expiry date

During this period, you stay under your current status of residence. People in the special period are not overstayers, even after the original expiry date has passed. If you applied at the counter, your residence card will have the notation "申請中" (application pending) on the back.

<h2 id="change">If your job or family situation has changed: apply for a change of status</h2>

If your activities in Japan have changed, apply for a **Change of Status of Residence (在留資格変更許可申請)**. Examples include changing jobs to work that your current status does not allow, getting a job after finishing your studies, or marrying a Japanese national.

If your activities no longer match your current [status of residence](/visa/guides/japan-visa-types/), an extension will not be approved. A change application filed by the expiry date is also covered by the special period.

<h2 id="denied">If your extension or change is denied</h2>

If your extension or change is denied, you may be allowed to change to **Designated Activities (特定活動)** status so you can prepare to leave Japan. However, this is not guaranteed, and the length of stay allowed differs from person to person.

You can ask immigration for the reason for the denial. Check the reason and your current expiry date, and contact immigration right away about what to do next.

<h2 id="expired">If your period of stay has already expired</h2>

If your period of stay expires without an application, you become an overstayer. You can be subject to deportation and to criminal penalties. If you are deported, in principle you cannot enter Japan for at least 5 years.

Even if your period of stay has already expired, the following systems exist.

**Departure Order System (出国命令制度)**<br>
This system allows overstayers who meet certain conditions to leave Japan without being detained. The conditions include either reporting to immigration on your own, or notifying immigration of your intention to leave by the legally specified stage after an immigration violation investigation has begun, and having no grounds for deportation other than overstaying. If you leave under this system, in principle you cannot re-enter Japan for 1 year. However, if you notified immigration of your intention to leave after the violation investigation had begun, and you try to re-enter with Temporary Visitor (短期滞在) status, the period is in principle 5 years. Whether you can use this system also depends on your past immigration record, criminal record and other factors.

**Special Permission to Stay (在留特別許可)**<br>
For people who are subject to deportation, the Minister of Justice may, as an exception, allow them to stay after considering their life in Japan, family relationships and other circumstances. Since June 10, 2024, certain people can also apply for Special Permission to Stay. However, there are conditions on who can apply and when, and an application does not guarantee approval.

If you realize your period of stay has expired, do not try to decide on your own. Contact immigration as soon as possible.

<h2 id="help">Not sure what to do?</h2>

What you can do when your period of stay is about to expire depends on whether you will continue the same activities, whether they have changed, and how many days you have left. What matters most is applying by the expiry date.<br>
