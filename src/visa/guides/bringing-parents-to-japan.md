<h2 id="dependent">Parents are not eligible for Dependent status</h2>

The spouse and children of a person working in Japan may be able to live in Japan under the [Dependent (家族滞在)](/visa/guides/bringing-family-to-japan/) status of residence. However, parents are not eligible for Dependent status.

This is the same even if you are a permanent resident or the spouse of a Japanese national. Japan does not have a generally available status of residence that allows foreign nationals to bring their parents to live with them.

<h2 id="hsp">Highly Skilled Professionals can bring their parents under certain conditions</h2>

Highly Skilled Professionals, including those qualifying under the Special Highly Skilled Professionals (J-Skip) system, may be able to bring their own parents or their spouse's parents, including adoptive parents, to Japan under **Designated Activities (特定活動)** status if the applicable requirements are met.

- **Purpose:** to care for a child under the age of seven, including an adopted child, of the Highly Skilled Professional or their spouse, or to provide support during the pregnancy of the Highly Skilled Professional or their spouse
- **Household income:** at least ¥8 million per year. Only remuneration received by the Highly Skilled Professional and their spouse counts toward this requirement
- **Living arrangements:** the parent must live with the Highly Skilled Professional
- **Eligible parents:** the Highly Skilled Professional's parents and their spouse's parents cannot use this arrangement at the same time. Both parents on the same side of the family may qualify, provided each meets the applicable requirements

This arrangement is intended to support childcare or pregnancy, not to bring parents to Japan for the purpose of receiving care themselves.

The arrangement for parents of Highly Skilled Professionals is a system set up for people who meet these requirements. By contrast, elderly parent support, explained below, is not granted simply because fixed conditions are met. Each case is decided individually.

<h2 id="roshin">Support for an Elderly Parent (老親扶養)</h2>

In exceptional cases, an elderly parent may be permitted to stay in Japan under Designated Activities status on humanitarian grounds. This is commonly referred to in Japanese immigration practice as *rōshin fuyō* (老親扶養).

It is not a standard immigration category set out in a law or public notice (告示). The Immigration Services Agency has not published a specific set of approval criteria for these cases. Each application is assessed individually, and approval is generally considered difficult to obtain.

<h2 id="factors">Factors discussed in immigration practice</h2>

The following factors are mentioned in guidance published by immigration practitioners such as gyoseishoshi offices. They are not official review criteria published by the Immigration Services Agency, and meeting all of them does not guarantee approval.

- **Age:** being elderly is said to be considered. Practitioners sometimes refer to approximately 70 years of age as a general guideline, but this is not an official age requirement. Age alone does not decide the outcome
- **Availability of support in the home country:** even if the parent has a spouse, other children or other relatives, they may not realistically be able to provide support because of illness, financial reasons or other circumstances. It is said to be important to explain specifically why support is not available, not just whether relatives exist
- **Family circumstances:** if both parents are alive, they may be seen as able to support each other, and the need for support may be assessed more strictly. However, having both parents alive is not in itself a legal ground for refusal
- **Health:** if the parent needs support because of an illness or physical limitations, medical records or a doctor's certificate may help explain the situation. A medical certificate does not guarantee approval
- **Financial support in Japan:** whether the person in Japan can provide ongoing financial support and accommodation is said to be considered

<h2 id="apply">Applying for Designated Activities status</h2>

Unlike the usual way of inviting family members with a Certificate of Eligibility, the application process for elderly parent support must be assessed on a case-by-case basis. Immigration practitioners describe cases in which a parent enters Japan under Temporary Visitor status and subsequently applies to change to Designated Activities status.

However, under the proviso to Article 20(3) of the Immigration Control and Refugee Recognition Act, a change from Temporary Visitor status is permitted only when unavoidable special circumstances exist. Entering Japan as a visitor or being elderly does not, by itself, establish grounds for approval.

It is important to review the circumstances and carefully consider whether there may be grounds for an application before the parent travels to Japan.

If permission is granted, the period of stay and permitted activities are determined individually. Employment is generally not permitted under an elderly parent support arrangement.

<h2 id="visit">Short visits: Temporary Visitor status</h2>

If your parents simply want to visit you for a while rather than live with you, they may enter Japan under Temporary Visitor (短期滞在) status. The period of stay for Temporary Visitors is 15, 30 or 90 days, among others, and the period actually granted depends on nationality, the purpose of the visit and other factors.

Depending on their nationality, your parents may need to obtain a visa in advance at a Japanese embassy or consulate. In that case, you, as their child in Japan, may need to prepare documents such as a letter explaining the reason for the invitation (招へい理由書).

<h2 id="help">Need advice on bringing your parents to Japan?</h2>

Whether you can bring your parents to Japan depends greatly on your status of residence, your parents' age and health, and their family situation in your home country. What matters most is checking which options are available before your parents come to Japan.<br>
