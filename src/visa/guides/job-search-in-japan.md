<h2 id="check">Step 1: Check what work you are allowed to do</h2>

Before you start looking, check what work your [status of residence](/visa/guides/japan-visa-types/) allows. Look at "Restriction on Work" on the front of your Residence Card and the "Permission for activity other than that permitted" section on the back. For some statuses, you also need to check the Certificate of Designation attached to your passport.

| Status of Residence | Work you can do |
|---|---|
| Permanent Resident, Spouse or Child of Japanese National, Spouse or Child of Permanent Resident, Long-Term Resident | No restriction on the type of work under your status of residence |
| Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services, Specified Skilled Worker, etc. | Work within the scope allowed by your status |
| Student, Dependent | Generally not allowed to work, but with Permission to Engage in Activities Other Than That Permitted, you can work within the allowed scope |
| Designated Activities | Depends on the activities designated for you |

With the general Permission to Engage in Activities Other Than That Permitted, students and dependents can generally work up to 28 hours per week. If you work at more than one place, the hours are added together. Students can work up to 8 hours a day during long school holidays, such as summer vacation.

The general permission does not allow you to work at businesses regulated under Japan's entertainment business laws, such as pachinko parlors, snack bars and hostess bars. However, since October 1, 2026, people with Dependent status can work at amusement facilities such as pachinko parlors if they obtain individual permission (still within 28 hours per week).

**International students who want to work in Japan after graduation** generally need to change to a status of residence that matches the job. Eligibility is not determined solely by the type of employment contract. The immigration authorities review the job duties, your education and work experience, and the employment conditions. For some statuses, the connection between what you studied and the job is also reviewed.

Before applying, check whether the job meets the requirements of your current status or the status you plan to change to. Even with a job offer, a change of status may not be approved.

<h2 id="prepare">Step 2: Prepare your résumé and improve your Japanese</h2>

Japanese companies may ask for a résumé in the Japanese format (rirekisho). Write your education, work history and qualifications accurately. If you have passed the Japanese-Language Proficiency Test (JLPT), include the level you passed.

### Japanese language skills open up more job opportunities

If you want to find a job in Japan, improving your Japanese can make a big difference.

Better Japanese language skills give you access to a wider range of jobs, help you communicate your knowledge and experience in interviews, and make it easier to learn your duties after starting a new job. We strongly recommend taking the JLPT or a similar exam to demonstrate your Japanese language proficiency.

<h2 id="public">Step 3: Use the free public employment services</h2>

Start with the free consultation services run by the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare.

- **[Employment Service Centers for Foreigners](https://jsite.mhlw.go.jp/tokyo-foreigner/gaikokujin_center_goannai.html)** (in Japanese): There are four, in Tokyo, Nagoya, Osaka and Fukuoka. They support international students who want to work in Japan and people with a status of residence for professional occupations. Some centers also offer advice on the immigration procedures for starting a job
- **Support desks for international students**: Some Hello Work offices for new graduates have desks that help international students find jobs
- **Shinjuku Employment Support Center for Foreigners (Tokyo)**: For people with statuses such as Permanent Resident or Spouse or Child of Japanese National, and for international students looking for part-time work

You can also go to your local Hello Work office. Who can use each service and which languages are available differ, so please check each official website. If you are a student, also talk to your school's career center.

<h2 id="agency">Step 4: If you use a recruitment agency, check its license</h2>

You can also use job websites and recruitment agencies. If you use a recruitment agency, check that it has a job placement license. You can search on the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's [Human Resources Services Comprehensive Site](https://jinzai.hellowork.mhlw.go.jp/JinzaiWeb) (in Japanese).

**Be careful if someone says, "Pay us and we will find you a job"**<br>
With some exceptions, recruitment agencies in Japan cannot charge job seekers a placement fee. If you are asked to pay one, do not pay on the spot. Contact Hello Work or the Prefectural Labour Bureau.

<h2 id="offer">Step 5: After a job offer, check your status of residence</h2>

After you receive a job offer, check whether your current status allows that job. If you need to change your status, you must receive permission before you start the new job. Simply applying for a change does not allow you to start work that is only permitted under the new status.

Even when you move to the same type of job, some statuses require permission to change your status of residence. For example, with Specified Skilled Worker, you generally need this permission when you move to a different company, even in the same field.

For some statuses, you must notify the Immigration Services Agency within 14 days of leaving or changing your employer.

International students who have not found a job by graduation may be able to change to Designated Activities to continue job hunting, if they meet the requirements.

If you live outside Japan and do not yet have a Japanese status of residence, the process is different. Please see [How to Work in Japan as a Foreigner](/visa/guides/work-in-japan-from-abroad/).
