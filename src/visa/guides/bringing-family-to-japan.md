<h2 id="status">Your status of residence determines whether your family can join you</h2>

In most cases, the family of a person working in Japan lives in Japan under the **Dependent (家族滞在)** status. Dependent status is available, in principle, when you hold one of the following statuses and meet the required conditions:

- Professor, Artist, Religious Activities, Journalist
- Highly Skilled Professional, Business Manager, Legal / Accounting Services, Medical Services, Researcher, Instructor
- Engineer / Specialist in Humanities / International Services, Intra-company Transferee, Nursing Care, Entertainer, Skilled Labor, Specified Skilled Worker (ii)
- Cultural Activities, Student

Even among people who "work in Japan," whether your family can join you depends on which [status of residence](/visa/guides/japan-visa-types/) you work under.

<h2 id="who">Who can join you: a spouse and children you support</h2>

The Dependent status covers a **spouse and children whom you support**. It assumes that your income or other resources can cover your family's living costs.

"Spouse" here means a person you are legally married to. In principle, a common-law partner is not covered by the Dependent status. However, even if someone does not qualify for Dependent status, a different status of residence may be granted depending on the circumstances.

Parents and siblings are also not covered by the Dependent status. [Bringing a parent](/visa/guides/bringing-parents-to-japan/) is only possible in very limited exceptional cases.

The period of stay is set for each person and does not exceed 5 years.

<h2 id="not-eligible">Statuses that cannot bring family under the Dependent status</h2>

People with a status that is not on the list above cannot bring their family under the Dependent status. For example:

- **Specified Skilled Worker (i) (特定技能1号):** in principle, you cannot bring your family under the Dependent status. Once you move to Specified Skilled Worker (ii), you can bring your spouse and children under the Dependent status
- **Technical Intern Training (技能実習):** a spouse or children supported by a technical intern trainee are not eligible for the Dependent status

If you plan to work for a company in Japan, first check with the company which status of residence you will work under.

<h2 id="special">Statuses with special rules for family members</h2>

With some statuses, family members can live in Japan under a framework other than the Dependent status.

- **Highly Skilled Professional (高度専門職):** if certain conditions are met, the spouse can work under **Designated Activities (特定活動)** status in jobs that fall under research, education, or Engineer / Specialist in Humanities / International Services, among others. The conditions include living with the Highly Skilled Professional and receiving pay equal to or more than what a Japanese national would receive. For this specific type of work, the education and work-experience requirements that apply to regular work statuses do not apply. This does not mean the spouse can do any kind of work
- **Digital Nomad:** a spouse and children who come with the nomad receive Designated Activities status. Like the nomad, they can stay in Japan for up to 6 months

<h2 id="steps">How to bring your family: the steps</h2>

When your family comes from abroad, the usual steps are:

1. Apply to immigration in Japan for a **Certificate of Eligibility (在留資格認定証明書)**
2. Once the certificate is issued, your family applies for a visa at the Japanese embassy or consulate in the country where they live
3. With the visa, your family enters Japan

Depending on the airport, the residence card is issued on arrival. If your family enters through another airport, the residence card is issued after they register their place of residence.

Your family can come at the same time as you, or later, after you have started living in Japan.

The main documents for the application are:

- Documents proving your family relationship (such as a marriage certificate or birth certificate)
- A copy of your residence card or passport
- Documents proving your job and income

<h2 id="transfer-or-self">Company transfer vs. working for yourself: what is different</h2>

Whether you are transferred by a company or work for yourself, your status of residence decides whether you can bring your family. The difference is in the **documents proving your job and income**.

- **Employees and people transferred by a company:** prove your job and income with documents such as a certificate of employment and documents showing your salary
- **Self-employed people:** show what your business does with documents such as a business license, and show that you can support your family with documents showing your income

If you live in Japan, you may prove your income with a resident tax certificate (住民税の課税証明書) or a tax payment certificate (納税証明書). If you have recently arrived in Japan and cannot obtain these tax certificates, check with the immigration office about which alternative documents you should submit.

With a company transfer, the company usually prepares many of the documents. If you run your own business, you will need to provide evidence that your business income can cover your family's living costs.

<h2 id="work">Can your family work in Japan?</h2>

Family members with Dependent status are not automatically permitted to work. To work, they need **Permission to Engage in Activity Other Than That Permitted under the Status of Residence Previously Granted (資格外活動許可)**.

With the general type of this permission (comprehensive permission), they can, in principle, work up to 28 hours a week. However, they cannot work at premises where businesses classified as *fuzoku eigyo* (風俗営業), certain adult-oriented businesses, or specified late-night entertainment businesses operate under Japanese law. This restriction applies regardless of the type of work performed, including cleaning or dishwashing. It covers certain bars and pachinko parlors, not just adult entertainment businesses.
