<div class="guide-summary">

To get permanent residency, you must meet three requirements: good conduct, the ability to support yourself independently, and your permanent residence being in Japan's interest.<br>
In principle, you must have lived in Japan continuously for at least 10 years, including at least 5 years with a work or residence-based status.<br>
New standards, such as those on pension and Japanese language ability, apply to applications filed on or after April 1, 2027. The new income standard already applies to applications filed on or after October 1, 2026, and to applications filed on or after April 1, 2026 that were still pending on October 1, 2026.<br>
For applications received on or after October 1, 2026, the fee is, in principle, ¥200,000.

<h2 id="what">What is permanent residency?</h2>

With permission for permanent residence, your status of residence becomes **Permanent Resident (永住者)**. Permanent residents have no limit on their period of stay and no restrictions on the type of work they can do.

However, your nationality does not change. Getting Japanese nationality is a separate procedure called naturalization (帰化).

<h2 id="requirements">The three requirements for permanent residency</h2>

Under the law, you must meet the following three requirements:

- **Good conduct:** you obey the law and live without doing anything in daily life that society would criticize
- **Ability to support yourself independently:** you can support yourself with your own income or assets, now and in the future
- **Your permanent residence is in Japan's interest:** for example, you have lived in Japan for a long time, you pay your taxes and social insurance contributions properly, and you comply with obligations such as notifications

The first two requirements do not apply to spouses and children of Japanese nationals, permanent residents and special permanent residents.

<h2 id="ten-years">In principle, at least 10 years of residence in Japan</h2>

For permanent residency, the following are considered in principle:

- You have lived in Japan continuously for at least 10 years
- Of those, you have lived in Japan continuously for at least 5 years with a work status (excluding Technical Intern Training, Specified Skilled Worker (i) and others) or a residence-based status
- You hold the longest period of stay available for your current [status of residence](/visa/guides/japan-visa-types/)

Under the revised guideline, Employment for Skill Development (育成就労), Intra-company Transferee and some Designated Activities are also excluded from the work statuses that count toward these 5 years.

Time spent in Japan as a student or with Dependent status counts toward the 10 years, but not toward the 5 years with a work or residence-based status. Periods spent under certain Designated Activities statuses excluded by the revised guideline, such as those for job hunting, do not count toward the 10 years either.

<h2 id="exceptions">Who can apply with fewer than 10 years (exceptions)</h2>

The following people may be granted permanent residency even if they have lived in Japan for fewer than 10 years.

The list below shows the new standards, which in principle apply to applications filed on or after April 1, 2027. For applications filed by March 31, 2027, the previous exceptions apply: spouses of Japanese nationals, permanent residents and special permanent residents need "a genuine marriage of at least 3 years and at least 1 year of continuous residence in Japan," and their biological children and similar need "at least 1 year of continuous residence in Japan."

- **Spouses of Japanese nationals, permanent residents and special permanent residents:** a genuine marriage that has lasted at least 5 years, and at least 3 years of continuous residence in Japan
- **Their biological children and similar:** at least 3 years of continuous residence in Japan
- **Long-Term Residents (定住者):** at least 5 years of continuous residence in Japan
- **People recognized as refugees and similar:** at least 5 years of continuous residence in Japan after recognition
- **Highly skilled foreign professionals:** at least 3 years of residence while keeping 70 points or more, or at least 1 year while keeping 80 points or more
- **People recognized as having contributed to Japan:** at least 5 years of residence in Japan

<h2 id="changes">What changes with the October 2026 revision?</h2>

The guideline was revised on October 1, 2026. The main changes are:

- **Income:** Immigration looks at whether your household's annual income has consistently been above the average for Japanese households of the same size. No specific amount is given. The income of family members who do not have a work status, such as those with Dependent status, is not included in household income
- **Pension:** Your expected future pension is compared with the level you would receive after working for 30 years and paying into the employees' pension insurance. You can make up any shortfall with assets such as savings
- **Japanese language ability:** Japanese language ability at B1 level or higher under Japan's Framework of Reference for Japanese Language Education is considered. For highly skilled foreign professionals and their families, and for people who have received a total of 6 years or more of elementary, junior high or high school education as defined in the School Education Act (学校教育法), Japanese language ability may not be considered in the review
- **Understanding of Japanese rules:** Immigration checks that you have a certain level of understanding, mainly of the content of the government's "Guidebook on Living and Working"
- **Time spent outside Japan:** If, within the past 10 years, you left Japan without a reasonable reason for 6 months or more in a single trip, or for a total of 2 years and 6 months or more, this is, in principle, viewed negatively
- **Children's school attendance:** If you are raising school-age children, they are expected to attend elementary or junior high school
- **Taxes and social insurance:** Having had a delinquency disposition (滞納処分) for unpaid taxes or contributions in the past is also, in principle, viewed negatively

<h2 id="timing">When do the new standards apply?</h2>

Which standards apply depends on the date you file your application.

| Application date | Standards applied |
|---|---|
| By March 31, 2026 | In principle, the previous guideline |
| April 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027 | New standards on income and on not becoming a public burden (including applications still pending on October 1, 2026). Otherwise, the previous guideline |
| On or after April 1, 2027 | The new guideline |

There is also a transitional measure for the longest period of stay. People with a 3-year period of stay who apply by March 31, 2027 are treated as holding the longest period of stay. From April 1, 2027, the same treatment applies only to a first application that meets certain conditions.

<h2 id="fee">Fee and processing time</h2>

For applications received on or after October 1, 2026, the fee for permanent residency is, in principle, ¥200,000 (previously ¥10,000). If certain requirements are met, it may be reduced to ¥20,000. The fee is paid when your application is approved.

The review takes a long time and can take close to a year.

<h2 id="help">Thinking about permanent residency?</h2>

Whether you can get permanent residency is decided not only by how many years you have lived in Japan, but by your life here as a whole, including your income, pension, how you have paid your taxes, and your Japanese language ability. What matters most is checking the requirements and starting to prepare several years before you apply.<br>
