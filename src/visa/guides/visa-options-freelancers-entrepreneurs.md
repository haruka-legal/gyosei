**In short:** Japan has no status of residence called a "freelance visa." To work as a freelancer, start a business, or keep working remotely in Japan, you need a [status of residence](/visa/guides/japan-visa-types/) that matches how you will work. The main options are **Business Manager** for running your own business, the **startup visa** for preparing to start one, **Engineer / Specialist in Humanities / International Services** for working under contracts with organizations in Japan, **Digital Nomad** for continuing your work from abroad, and **Artist** for artists. Statuses such as **Permanent Resident** and **Spouse or Child of Japanese National** have no restrictions on the type of work.

<h2 id="no-freelance-visa">Japan has no "freelance visa"</h2>

If you search for "freelance visa" in English, it can look as if Japan has one, but Japan has no status of residence called "freelance."

Japan's statuses of residence are decided by the activities you will do in Japan. What matters is not whether you are an employee or a freelancer, but what work you do, who your contract is with, and where you do the work.

<h2 id="business-manager">Live in Japan by running your own business: Business Manager</h2>

If you set up a company or run a business as a sole proprietor in Japan, **[Business Manager](/visa/guides/how-to-move-to-japan/#business) (経営・管理)** is the status of residence to consider.

The criteria changed significantly on October 16, 2025. In principle, you now need to meet the following conditions:

- For a company, the capital or total investment must be at least ¥30 million. For a sole proprietorship, the total amount invested in the business, such as securing a business office, staff salaries (for one year) and equipment, must be at least ¥30 million.
- You must hire at least one full-time employee. Only the following can be counted: Japanese nationals, special permanent residents, permanent residents, spouses or children of Japanese nationals, spouses or children of permanent residents, and long-term residents.
- Either you or a full-time employee must have Japanese language proficiency equivalent to B2 or higher under Japan's Framework of Reference for Japanese Language Education. The Japanese-Language Proficiency Test (JLPT) N2 or higher is one accepted way to show this, but other forms of evidence may also qualify.
- You must have at least 3 years of experience in business management or administration, or a degree equivalent to a master's or higher in business management or in a field of technology or knowledge needed for the business.
- Your business plan must be checked by a professional holding one of the following Japanese qualifications: small and medium enterprise management consultant (中小企業診断士), certified public accountant (公認会計士), or certified tax accountant (税理士).
- You must have a business office in Japan. In principle, using your home as your business office is not allowed.

The full-time employee who meets the Japanese language requirement does not have to be one of the people listed above for the headcount. For example, an employee with Engineer / Specialist in Humanities / International Services status can also meet the Japanese language requirement.

There are many conditions, and preparation takes time and money. Rather than applying after setting up your company, it is important to check whether you can meet the conditions while you are still planning.

<h2 id="startup">Start by preparing your business: the startup visa</h2>

If it is difficult to meet all the Business Manager conditions right away, there is a program called the **Program for Promoting Business Startups by Foreign Nationals (外国人起業活動促進事業)**, commonly called the startup visa. The status of residence is **Designated Activities (特定活動)**.

You can prepare to start a business in Japan for up to 2 years, under the management and support of a local government or private business certified by the Minister of Economy, Trade and Industry. Once you are ready, you aim to change your status to Business Manager.

You can only use this program where a local government or private business runs it. First, check whether it is available in the area where you want to start your business.

<h2 id="contracts">Work under contracts with organizations in Japan: Engineer / Specialist in Humanities / International Services</h2>

Engineers, translators and others who work under outsourcing contracts instead of as employees may still be able to use the **Engineer / Specialist in Humanities / International Services (技術・人文知識・国際業務)** status.

This status covers activities based on a contract with a public or private organization in Japan. It includes not only employment contracts but also outsourcing and similar contracts, and you can have contracts with more than one organization.

However, an outsourcing contract alone does not guarantee approval. The main points to check are:

- The work is based on a contract with a public or private organization in Japan.
- The contract is a continuing one with specific organizations, not a one-off job.
- You receive pay equal to or more than what a Japanese national would receive for the same work.
- The work is covered by the status, and you meet the required standards for education or work experience.

If you only work for clients abroad, this status may not apply. Also, when a contract ends or you sign a new contract, you may need to notify the Immigration Services Agency within 14 days.

<h2 id="digital-nomad">Keep your work abroad and work remotely: Digital Nomad</h2>

People employed by a company abroad, or who provide services to clients abroad, can stay in Japan and keep working remotely under the **Digital Nomad** program. The main conditions are:

- Your own annual income is ¥10 million or more.
- You hold the nationality of an [eligible country or region](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/content/001416527.pdf) (see the column "特定活動53号（デジタルノマド）" — Designated Activities No. 53, Digital Nomad — in this PDF).
- You have private medical or travel insurance covering your entire planned stay in Japan, including death, injury and illness. Coverage for medical treatment due to injury or illness must be at least ¥10 million.

However, you can stay for a maximum of 6 months, the stay cannot be extended, and no residence card is issued. You also cannot work as an employee of a company in Japan. This program is not a way to live in Japan long-term; it is for experiencing life in Japan.

<h2 id="artist">Live in Japan as a writer or painter: Artist</h2>

Composers, painters, writers and others who live in Japan while earning income by creating works or writing can use the **Artist (芸術)** status. It covers artistic activities that come with income. Performing in concerts or on stage falls under a different status, Entertainer (興行).

When you apply, you need documents showing your past artistic activities, what you will do in Japan, and your expected income. Even if you have no work contract, you need to explain your activities and expected income.

<h2 id="spouse">Spouses of Japanese nationals or permanent residents: statuses with no limits on the type of work</h2>

People married to a Japanese national or a permanent resident, and others, may be granted statuses such as **Spouse or Child of Japanese National (日本人の配偶者等)** or **Spouse or Child of Permanent Resident (永住者の配偶者等)** if they meet the requirements.

These statuses, as well as Permanent Resident and Long-Term Resident, have no work restrictions based on the status of residence. So you can work as a freelancer or run your own business.

However, getting married does not automatically give you a status of residence. Also, Dependent (家族滞在) is a different status, and in principle it does not allow work, so be careful not to confuse them.

<h2 id="choose">Not sure which option fits you?</h2>

How you can freelance or start a business in Japan depends on your work, who you contract with, your income, and your funds. Even among "freelancers," the status of residence you can use can be completely different. What matters most is checking which status of residence fits how you work before you start.
